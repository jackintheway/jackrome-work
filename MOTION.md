# Motion on this site

Decided 2026-08-22, with Jack. This file exists so the question does not get
reopened from scratch, and so the next session knows what is settled, what was
deliberately taken from elsewhere, and what is not allowed to happen yet.

Short version: **the site stays calm. Scroll-linked motion is admitted in exactly
one place, the Wayspace landing, and not until after the DNS cutover.**

---

## What prompted this

Nate Herk published a Claude Code skill called ScrollCraft, MIT licensed, at
`github.com/nateherkai/scroll-craft`. It builds scroll-driven landing pages where
the scroll position drives what is happening on screen: numbers counting, a map
filling in, cards moving laterally. It runs an interview before it generates
anything, produces or ingests assets, writes the page, then screenshots itself at
successive scroll positions to check its own work.

It is well made and worth reading. The question was whether any of it belongs here.

## The decision: not adopted as a build path

ScrollCraft is a generator, not a library. It authors the page itself from a brief,
ships its own engine (`engine/scrollcraft.js` and `engine/scrollcraft.css`, which its
own instructions say are never edited per project), and themes off its own six
tokens: `--sc-canvas`, `--sc-surface`, `--sc-ink`, `--sc-ink-soft`, `--sc-accent`,
`--sc-accent-ink`. It needs Node 18, a full ffmpeg build, and playwright-core. Its
default asset is generated video, scrubbed by the scroll.

Three reasons it does not fit:

1. **It would be a second design system.** This site's tokens are copied verbatim
   from `../wayspace-design-system/`, which is the source of truth. ScrollCraft's
   engine reads its own namespace. Running both means two systems that drift.
2. **It brings a toolchain.** The standing commitment here is static HTML, CSS, and
   vanilla JS, with no build step. `netlify.toml` sets `command = ""` on purpose.
3. **Its assets are generated by default; this site's are real.** Wayspace is a
   gathering of work Jack actually made. Generated filler is the wrong material for
   a room whose whole point is that the work exists.

There is also a register problem, and it is worth naming because it is the part that
generalizes. ScrollCraft is built for hook-and-retain: get them in the first three
seconds, keep them scrolling. That is a legitimate craft goal for a product launch.
It is not what this site is for. **The technique is portable. The register is not.**

Do not install it. It stays useful as reading.

## What is taken from it anyway

Three things, none of which need its engine. Two have nothing to do with motion and
are the cheapest wins here.

### 1. Verify by scrolling, not just by page

`~/.claude/skills/verify-site/` already serves the site, drives headless Chrome, and
screenshots. It checks a page as a single state. A page with any scroll-linked
behavior has no single state, and the failures live between frames.

The extension, when there is anything to verify: shoot a strip of screenshots across
successive scroll positions rather than one at the top, and shoot it three times: at
desktop width, at 390px, and again with reduced motion forced. That third run is the
one that catches a page which only works if the animation runs.

This costs no new dependency. Chrome is already being driven.

### 2. The feeling curve

ScrollCraft's rule, stated plainly: one peak, given the most room, with a quieter
beat immediately before it. No two adjacent sections carrying the same feeling. An
ending that resolves rather than trails off.

That is editing-room pacing, and it applies to a page with no motion at all. It is
worth holding when writing or ordering any page here, `/production` included.

### 3. One sentence per page: what must the visitor believe by the end?

ScrollCraft asks this in its interview and refuses a feature list as an answer.
`CLAUDE.md` records a great many decisions about this site and does not contain a
sentence like that for any page.

That gap is worth closing, and the place to start is `/production`, because it is the
one page with no copy written yet. Not "what sections does it need." One sentence:
what does someone believe when they leave.

**This is not an argument that the site needs more intake.** Jack's intake for
jackrome.work has run for years and produced an entire design system. It is one
specific question that has not been written down, and writing it down is cheap.

## The register, settled

**Calm, spacious, trust-building.** That holds for `/`, `/about`, `/ai-enablement`,
`/ai-portfolio`, and `/production` when it is built. Those pages sell work and ask
for a conversation. Motion that pulls at the reader works against that.

The current state is deliberate and should be understood as a decision rather than
an unfinished job. There are no `@keyframes` anywhere in `css/`. Every transition on
the site is a hover or focus state, 110ms or 180ms, on `transform` and `color`.
`css/site.css` says it directly: every transition on this site is decoration.

**`/wayspace` is the one exception, and only because of what it is.** It is the
creative doorway, not a service page. The work in it is visual and it is Jack's own.
A room can behave differently from a storefront.

## Where the motion actually goes

`CLAUDE.md`, under "Still open on Wayspace," already reserves this as item 5: the
floating puzzle art, drawn as one puzzle and pulled apart so the tabs match, layered
over the room list, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`. That open item is where
scroll-linked motion lives. Nothing new needs inventing to make room for it.

**What makes this Jack's rather than borrowed: the artwork is already built in depth
planes.** `_source/artwork/wayspace-cover.JPEG` separates cleanly into layers that
already sit at different distances:

| Plane | Contents |
|---|---|
| Sky | The orange to yellow gradient. Furthest back, barely moves. |
| Trees | The two green and yellow figures with eyes, flanking left and right. |
| Spire | The blue central tower. |
| Bridge | The curved blue and yellow span across the middle. |
| Flower | The yellow bloom and the spiral carrying the wordmark. |
| Water | The blue river and the buoys, nearest the viewer. |
| Butterflies | Scattered across at least three different depths. |

Jack has already animated exactly these planes, in Premiere, for the album
visualizers: the spiral turning, the trees advancing, the water receding. So this is
not a new visual idea. It is the existing one, moved into the browser, driven by the
reader's scroll instead of a timeline.

That is the whole argument for doing it at all. The scroll is not being added to
make the page sticky. It is the mechanism that lets a composition Jack already made
behave the way he already made it behave.

### Constraints for whoever builds it

- **The room list stays the navigation.** `wayspace.html` says this in a comment and
  it is not negotiable: an SVG that fails to load must not leave a visitor with no
  way into any room. The art layers over the list. It never replaces it.
- One `requestAnimationFrame` loop reading `scrollY`. Not a scroll event handler per
  layer.
- `transform: translate3d()` and `opacity` only. Never animate `width`, `height`,
  `top`, or `left`, and never `transition: all`.
- `will-change` off by default. Add it only where a measurement says it is needed.
- **Hard stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Not slowed. Layers render in place,
  static, composed. The reduced-motion block already at the bottom of
  `css/site.css` covers transitions; this needs its own explicit handling in JS.
- 390px, no horizontal scroll. Same rule as every other page. A wide composition
  needs a plan for narrow screens that is not "let it overflow."
- The art is decorative. `alt=""` on the layers, and the accessible name for every
  room still comes from the list underneath.

### The token gap

`css/tokens/spacing.css` has `--dur-fast: 110ms` and `--dur: 180ms`, plus
`--ease-out` and `--ease-pop`. Those are button durations. There is no vocabulary
for anything at narrative scale and no depth or layer scale at all.

If motion becomes part of the system rather than one page's exception, the durations
and a depth scale belong in `../wayspace-design-system/` and get copied down.
**Do not edit `css/tokens/` directly.** Change the system and re-copy.

## Not before the cutover

This is the part that matters most right now.

Squarespace renews 2026-09-15. Target for the DNS cutover is roughly 2026-09-01, so
there is a rollback window inside a term already paid for. As of this file, `/production`
is unbuilt and returns 404 behind links in the nav of every page on the site.

**Nothing in this file gets built before the cutover.** `/production`, the orphaned
page decisions, and the `/creative-portfolio` redirect are the critical path. Motion
is a good idea with no deadline attached to it, which makes it exactly the kind of
thing that eats a deadline that does exist.

Revisit after the cutover holds.
